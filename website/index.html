How the forecast works

One forecast, six auditable steps

The model starts with a broad economics-only expectation, updates it with one chamber-specific national polling input, and preserves uncertainty through every race and chamber simulation. Every margin is Democratic minus Republican: positive values favor Democrats and negative values favor Republicans.

1

Validate the inputs

Refresh Silver polling, five FRED series, and FEC/Clerk candidate records. Malformed or catastrophically stale data cannot replace the last valid forecast.

2

Set an economics-only prior

A standardized five-series economic index creates a deliberately broad pre-poll expectation. Its uncertainty includes the economic coefficient, input uncertainty, and a 3.5-point structural standard deviation.

3

Update national conditions once

The House collapses Silver’s adjusted poll universe into one weighted likelihood. The Senate uses Silver’s latest published likely-voter average once. The daily maintained-average history is never treated as independent repeated evidence.

4

Build every race prior

Partisan lean, incumbency, national conditions, shared regional movement, and local error produce a probability distribution of margins for each race.

5

Apply valid race averages

Only the latest verified Democratic-versus-Republican Silver average may update a race. Missing, third-party, or unresolved matchups remain fundamentals-only.

6

Simulate the election

In 10,000 correlated simulations, national and regional waves move races together. Control probability is the share reaching 218 House seats or 51 Senate seats.

How to read the probability

-- means Democrats won the House in that share of the model’s 10,000 simulated elections. It is not a predicted vote share, a percentage of seats, or a guarantee.

Model card

What the model assumes—and how it can fail

National polling likelihood

House: WeightedAdjustedPolls*
Senate: SilverPublishedAvgLatest*
one national observation per chamber

The House uses one weighted aggregate of Silver-adjusted polls; the Senate uses the latest published maintained average. Neither model multiplies the highly autocorrelated daily average history into repeated evidence.

Economy-only fundamentals prior

margin ~ Normal(-0.34 × econ_index, σ)
σ includes 3.5-point structural error

The economic composite informs the broad pre-poll expectation once. Polling then updates that prior; economics is not added again afterward.

Race posterior

marginᵣ ~ Student-t(fundamentalsᵣ, σᵣ)
SilverAvgLatestᵣ updates marginᵣ

Races without a validated Silver D-v-R average remain at their fundamentals posterior. Shared national and regional draws preserve chamber correlation; a historical common-error floor prevents false precision.

Data provenance

Polling likelihoods: Silver Bulletin maintained averages. Economics: FRED. Candidates: FEC. Incumbents: Clerk of the House. Snapshots retain URL, fetch time, source notice, and checksum.

Known limitations

  • Silver's weighting model is external and is not reproduced or independently audited here.
  • The economic coefficient is deliberately weak and based on few comparable midterms.
  • Ambiguous, third-party, and uncovered races stay fundamentals-only.
  • House structural and Senate update validation do not directly validate Silver’s 2026 averages.
  • No MRP, fundraising, endorsements, or subjective candidate scores.
